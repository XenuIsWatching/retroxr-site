## Major new features

### 🃏 The GBA e-Reader

Nintendo printed **dotcodes along the edges of trading cards** — whole small games, NES
titles, extra levels for Super Mario Advance 4 — and you swiped the card through a scanner
plugged into your Game Boy Advance.

v0.6.0 does that as a gesture. Push the reader into the handheld, pick up a card, and
**slide it through the slit in the cartridge's roof**, dotcode edge first. There is no bay
and nothing to seat — a card that could be seated would be caught half way through a swipe.

All three revisions are here — Card e-Reader, Card e-Reader+ and the USA e-Reader — and
they are not interchangeable, because cards are region-locked and a reader answers a
foreign card with its own Region Error screen.

<Aside type="caution" title="Your cards must carry their No-Intro names">
	A card is one or two `.raw` strips, and the strips are paired **by filename** — which
	edge a strip belongs on is recorded nowhere in the file itself. Keep the No-Intro forms
	(`(Strip 1)`/`(Strip 2)`, or `(Long Strip)`/`(Short Strip)`) or a two-strip card is
	silently split into two half-cards.
</Aside>

Full detail, including where the reader's own dump goes, on the new
[Card e-Reader](/guide/platforms/e-reader/) page.

### 🎮 An N64 controller, and the paks that go in it

The Nintendo 64 got a modelled controller — and, more to the point, the **expansion port in
its back**. Spawn a pak, push it in, and the core is told what is there:

- **Controller Pak** — 32 KiB of saves, counted in pages like every N64 game counts them.
- **Rumble Pak** — force feedback, in the games that support it.
- **Transfer Pak** — socket a **Game Boy cartridge** into it for the games that read one.

Two things about saves are worth knowing. A Controller Pak keeps **its own file**, so a pak
carries its notes between controllers and machines rather than belonging to a game or a
slot. And a Transfer Pak's Game Boy save is the **cartridge's own** — play the cart in a
Game Boy, carry it to the Transfer Pak, same save, exactly as the hardware did.

The port is on the pad *receiver* as well, so a player on a real gamepad can use paks too.

See [Nintendo 64](/guide/platforms/nintendo-64/).

## Minor new features

- **The Expansion Pak** goes into the console, replacing the Jumper Pak, and **sets the
  core option for you** — the state of the machine is the setting. The N64 also has a new
  primitive model.
- **The FM Sound Unit** attaches to a Master System for the expanded soundtrack a number of
  Japanese releases shipped with, again setting the core options itself.
- **The Power Base Converter** goes into a Genesis and plays Master System cartridges. See
  [Sega Genesis](/guide/platforms/sega-genesis/).
