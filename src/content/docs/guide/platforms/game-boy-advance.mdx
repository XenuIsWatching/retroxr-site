The Game Boy Advance plays cartridges from your `roms/game_boy_advance` folder. What it
grew, and what is new in **v0.6.0**, is the **Card e-Reader**: a cartridge with a slit
through its roof that you slide printed cards through, edge first.

## The Card e-Reader

The e-Reader is a GBA cartridge with a scanner in it. Nintendo printed **dotcodes** along
the edges of trading cards — whole little games, Animal Crossing NES titles, extra levels
for Super Mario Advance 4 — and you swiped the card through the slot to load one.

In RetroXR you do exactly that. Push the reader into the handheld, then **take a card and
slide it through the slit**, dotcode edge first. There is no bay and nothing to seat: the
card is never held, because a card that could be seated would be captured half way through
a swipe.

<Aside type="note" title="Three readers, and they are not interchangeable">
	RetroXR carries all three revisions — **Card e-Reader** (Japan), **Card e-Reader+**
	(Japan) and **e-Reader (USA)**. A reader *is* its dump, and cards are region-locked: a
	Japanese reader answers an American card with its own Region Error screen, exactly as
	the hardware did. Spawn the one that matches your cards.
</Aside>

### The reader's program comes from your library

The e-Reader's cartridge dump is an **ordinary GBA ROM**, so RetroXR does not treat it as
firmware or ask you to install a second copy under a name of its choosing. Put it where you
would keep it anyway:

- `roms/game_boy_advance/` — the shelf it belongs on, or
- `roms/ereader/` — beside the cards it reads, which is also reasonable.

It is recognised by the **ROM's own header** — the GBA game code — not by its filename, so
renaming your dump does not hide it. The library then stops offering that file as a game,
since it is hardware rather than something you play.

## Card names must follow No-Intro

This is the one thing that will bite you. A card is one or two `.raw` dotcode strips, and
**RetroXR groups the strips of a card by filename**, using the No-Intro naming. Get the
names wrong and every two-strip card is silently split into two half-cards.

Both No-Intro conventions are understood:

```
<card> (Strip 1).raw     +  <card> (Strip 2).raw       two long strips
<card> (Long Strip).raw  +  <card> (Short Strip).raw   a long and a short
<card>.raw                                             a single strip
```

<Aside type="caution" title="Why the name matters and the file cannot tell us">
	Which **edge** a strip is printed on is not recorded anywhere — not in the dotcode
	itself (a card's per-block headers are byte-identical between its strips), not in the
	core, and not reliably in a scan. Only the strip's **size** is certain: 2912 bytes is a
	long strip and 1872 a short one.

	So the pairing has to come from the name. A card whose two strips are not recognised as
	a pair presents the wrong edge, and swiping it reads nothing.
</Aside>

Cards go in `roms/ereader/`. A `.gba` dump sitting in that folder is ignored by the card
grouping, which only ever looks at `.raw` — so keeping the reader beside its cards is safe.

## Which edge you present

Every e-Reader card is the same portrait trading card, so there is no card shape to work
out — only which of its edges carry a dotcode. A long strip goes on a **long (side)** edge
and a short strip on a **short (bottom)** edge, because a 2912-byte dotcode does not fit
across 63 mm of card.

Present an uncoded edge and nothing is read, which is what should happen — that is a card
offered the wrong way round, not a failure.

## Scanning, and what a good scan looks like

The scanner has to be **powered and waiting** for a card. In practice: start the e-Reader
software, choose its scan option, and swipe once it is asking for a card. A card offered to
an unpowered scanner is simply dropped.

A two-strip card is swiped twice — one strip, then the other — and the software tells you
when it is satisfied:

> Scanning of Dot Code(s) complete.

<Aside type="tip" title='"Already Scanned" means the strips were paired wrongly'>
	If a correctly-swiped second strip comes back as *Already Scanned*, that is the pairing
	being wrong rather than a read error. Check the two filenames against the No-Intro
	forms above.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="One power-up is one card">
	The card leaves the scanner when it powers down. A scan session is short — power up, a
	few sweeps, give up, power down — so swipe within that window rather than lining a card
	up in advance.
</Aside>
