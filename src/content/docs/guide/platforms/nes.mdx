The NES is one of the few machines in RetroXR wearing a **detailed shell** rather than a
procedural stand-in — and it is the only one that molds a working RF panel, which makes it
the best example of how a pre-composite console reaches a television.

## Two ways out, both live at once

This is the part worth understanding. The NES carries **two** outputs, and they work at the
same time:

- **A phono pair on its flank** — mono composite. Video plus a single audio channel.
- **An RF SWITCH jack on its back**, with a **CH3/CH4 slide switch** beside it.

You can run a composite lead from the flank to one television and an RF switch from the
back to another, and both sets will show the picture simultaneously. Neither output
disables the other.

## Route 1: composite

The simplest path, and the one to use if your television has a spare phono row.

<Steps>

1. Spawn a **Mono Composite Cable** from `SPAWN` → Objects.
2. Plug one end into the phono pair on the NES's flank.
3. Plug the other into any **Composite** block on the back of the set.
4. Set the TV's **Source** to that input.

</Steps>

<Image src={avPanel} alt="The back panel of a television: four blocks labeled Composite 1 to Composite 4 with yellow video and white and red audio sockets, and a separate Antenna block with an RF IN socket." />

Note it is *mono* composite — video plus one audio plug, not the yellow-white-red trio. It
seats in the same row; there is simply one fewer plug.

## Route 2: RF, through the switch box

The way most people actually had it. The chain is:

```
NES  RF SWITCH jack  →  RXR-003 switch box  →  TV  ANT / RF IN
```

<Turntable
	src="/video/av/rf_switch.mp4"
	poster={rfPoster}
	alt="The RXR-003 RF switch rotating: a small white box with a coaxial lead and a phono lead coming out of it."
	caption="The RXR-003 RF switch. Phono in from the console, coax out to the set's antenna socket."
/>

<Steps>

1. Spawn an **RF Switch (RXR-003)** from `SPAWN` → Objects.
2. Plug its phono lead into the NES's **RF SWITCH** jack.
3. Plug its coax lead into the television's **ANT / RF IN** socket.
4. Set the TV's **Source** to the antenna input.
5. **Tune the set** to channel 3 or 4 — whichever the console's slide switch is set to.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution" title="The channel has to match">
	On the antenna input, the console's own CH3/CH4 switch decides whether there is a
	picture at all. A set tuned to the other channel shows **snow** — which is correct
	behavior, not a bug. Either move the slide switch on the console or tune the set.
</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="The ANT IN on the switch is not wired yet">
	The RXR-003 has an antenna passthrough input, as the real ones did, so a genuine
	antenna and a console could share one socket. It does nothing at present.
</Aside>

## The front panel

<Image src={frontAnnotated} alt="The front of the NES with three things circled: the POWER button marked 1, the RESET button marked 2, and the two controller ports marked 3." />

**① POWER** · **② RESET** · **③ two controller ports**

The **POWER button gives off its own red light** when the machine is on, rather than being
painted red — so a console that is on reads as on from across the room.

### It sounds like one

v0.4.0 added recorded hardware sounds to this machine, taken from a real NES-001: the
power and reset buttons, the controller buttons, a controller plug going into a port, the
CH3/CH4 switch, a cartridge going in and coming out, and the cradle latching and
unlatching.

## Cartridges, and the lid

<Turntable
	src="/video/nes/nes_cart.mp4"
	poster={cartPoster}
	alt="An NES cartridge model rotating, showing its label face and the ridged back."
	caption="An NES cartridge. It goes in label-up, and the bay is front-loading."
/>

The NES is front-loading, and the bay sits behind a **hinged flap**. You cannot put a
cartridge in — or take one out — while the flap is down.

<Image src={openAnnotated} alt="The NES with its front flap raised, circled and marked 1, revealing the dark cartridge bay behind it, circled and marked 2." />

**① the flap**, swung up · **② the cartridge bay** behind it

### Opening it

As of v0.4.0 the flap is a **poke**: put a fingertip under it and lift. No grip, no
trigger.

<Aside type="caution" title="A leftover trigger glyph">
	The flap still shows a trigger hint from when it took the trigger. Ignore it — poking
	is what works. It is a [known issue](https://github.com/XenuIsWatching/RetroXR/releases)
	in v0.4.0.
</Aside>

<Steps>

1. Poke the flap up.
2. Spawn a game from `SPAWN` → **Games** and push it into the bay. It goes in label
   up.
3. **Press the cradle down.** The NES's bay is sprung: a cartridge sitting in it is not
   yet connected, and the console cannot see the game until you push the cradle down and
   it latches — the clunk everyone remembers. Press it again to unlatch and pop the
   cartridge back up.
4. Press **POWER**.

</Steps>

You do not have to do the flap by hand every time: **inserting or removing a cartridge
drives it automatically**. Opening it yourself is for when you want to look, or to get a
cart out that you did not spawn with.

## The controller

Two controller ports, so two pads for two players. If you would rather use a real gamepad,
spawn a **Controller Receiver** and plug that into a port instead — see
[controllers and peripherals](/guide/playing/controllers/).

There is also a **Zapper** light gun for the games that used one.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | Usually means |
| --- | --- |
| **Snow** on the antenna input | The set is tuned to the wrong channel for the console's CH3/CH4 switch |
| **Nothing at all** | The set is on a different source, or the plug is not seated |
| Picture but **no sound** | Mono composite has one audio plug — check it is in, and check the set's volume |
