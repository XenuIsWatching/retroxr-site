The PlayStation is the platform that best shows what RetroXR does with saves. Your save is
not a hidden file in a folder — it is on a **memory card**, and the card is an object you
hold, slot in, and can lose.

## Before anything: the BIOS

The PlayStation will not boot without its BIOS, and RetroXR cannot ship it. Open `CORES` →
**BIOS / Extras** and pick PlayStation to see exactly which files it wants.

<Image src={biosPsx} alt="The BIOS / Extras view for PlayStation, listing scph5500.bin, scph5501.bin, scph5502.bin and psxonpsp660.bin with their required or optional status and per-file download buttons." />

The filenames have to match exactly — `scph5500.bin`, `scph5501.bin`, `scph5502.bin` and
`psxonpsp660.bin`. A file that is right in every way except its name will not be found.

If you run a [RomM](https://romm.app) server holding your firmware, RetroXR can pull these
down directly. Otherwise copy them in the usual way — see
[adding your games](/guide/adding-games/) and [BIOS files](/guide/cores/bios/).

**① The connector edge** goes into the console's card slot. **② The label** names the
card, which is how you tell two of them apart on a shelf.

### Using one

<Steps>

1. Spawn a **memory card** from `SPAWN` → **Objects**.

2. Push it into one of the console's two card slots.

3. Play. The game saves to that card, in that slot.

</Steps>

Because a card is a real object rather than a setting, the things you could do with a real
one work here:

- **Keep several saves on one card**, until it runs out of blocks.
- **Move a save from one card to another.**
- **Delete a save** to free blocks.
- **Back a card up** to your RomM server.

There is a manager for all of this, reachable from the card itself: point at it and press
the **menu button**.

<Aside type="tip" title="If a game cannot find your save">
	Saves are per card, and per slot. The usual cause is a different card in the slot, or
	the right card in the wrong slot. Check the card before suspecting the emulator.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="You cannot delete a save while the console is on">
	Power the machine down first. It is the same rule cartridge saves follow — see
	[game saves and backups](/guide/connecting/saves/).
</Aside>

### <RommMark />Backing a card up to RomM

A card you can lose is a card worth backing up, and this is the reason to connect a
[RomM](https://romm.app) server even if you copy your games across by hand.

<Steps>

1. [Connect RomM](/guide/connecting/romm/).

2. Back the card up from its own manager.

3. The card's contents live on your server, not only on the headset.

</Steps>

That matters more here than for most saves: on a Quest you cannot safely copy save files
back **in** from a PC — files pushed from a computer land owned by the shell rather than
the app, and RetroXR can no longer write to them. RomM is the route that avoids the
problem entirely.

Full detail on [game saves and backups](/guide/connecting/saves/).

## Discs

Spawn a game from `SPAWN` → **Cartridges** and it arrives as a disc rather than a
cartridge. Open the console's lid, set the disc in the tray, close it, and power on.

Multi-disc games work — RetroXR retrieves the right image for each disc rather than
handing every disc the first one, which was a real bug fixed in
[v0.3.0](/blog/).

<Aside type="note" title="A procedural stand-in">
	The PlayStation is one of the machines wearing a clean procedural stand-in rather than
	a detailed replica — fully playable, deliberately minimalist, like most of the hardware
	in the room. The memory cards above are the exception, and they are in.
</Aside>

## Controllers

Plug a pad into one of the two controller ports. For a real gamepad, spawn a **Controller
Receiver** and plug that in instead — see
[controllers and peripherals](/guide/playing/controllers/).

## Cores

The PlayStation has several libretro cores with different accuracy and speed trade-offs.
Pick the default in `CORES` → **Manager** — see [downloading and managing
cores](/guide/cores/downloading/).

:::caution[Needs an in-headset pass]
The memory card manager's exact controls, and the lid and tray interaction, are written
from the code and the v0.3.0 release notes rather than from a session in the headset.
:::
