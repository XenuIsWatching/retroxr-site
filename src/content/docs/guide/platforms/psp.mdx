## The power switch has to be held

This is the detail people trip over, and it is faithful: **the PSP's power switch is a
slider you hold, not a button you press.**

| Action | How long |
| --- | --- |
| **Power on** | Hold the switch at the end of its travel for about **one second** |
| **Power off** | Hold it for **three seconds** |

It **springs back** the moment you let go, exactly like the real one. A quick flick does
nothing at all — which is correct behavior, not an unresponsive control.

<Aside type="note" title="Why the two are different">
	Sony documents no minimum for switching a PSP on, and a real one lights up after about
	a second. For switching off, Sony's own instruction is "slide up and hold for more than
	three seconds". RetroXR uses both numbers.
</Aside>

## UMDs

Media is the **UMD**, and it does not go in a slot on top — it loads through a **door on
the back edge**.

<Steps>

1. Spawn a game from `SPAWN` → **Cartridges**.
2. Release the UMD door with the **latch** on the back edge.
3. Put the disc in and close the door.
4. Hold the power switch until it comes on.

</Steps>

The latch is a **sprung slide**, not a button — you push it along the edge, the door pops,
and the latch springs straight back. It is the same mechanism as the power switch, but
with no hold: it fires the moment it reaches the end of its throw. The shell prints
`OPEN ———▷` beside it with the arrow pointing the way it slides.

The latch reports intent rather than position, so the door **opens** every time. Pushing
it shut again is the door's own job.

## The controls

The full shell models each control as its own part: the ✕ ○ □ △ face diamond, the d-pad,
the analog nub, the L and R shoulders, Start, Select and Home, the volume ± and
sound/display keys, the slide power switch, the eject latch and the UMD door.

The face buttons follow the same glyph-position convention as everything else in RetroXR:

| PSP | RetroPad |
| --- | --- |
| **✕** | B — confirm |
| **○** | A — back |
| **□** | Y |
| **△** | X |

<Image src={pspControls} alt="The PSP with four system controls circled: the power switch marked 1, the OPEN latch marked 2, the volume keys marked 3, and the power LED marked 4." />

**① Power switch** — the slider you hold. **② OPEN** — the sprung UMD latch.
**③ Volume − / +**. **④ Power LED**.

The face buttons, d-pad and analog nub are the gameplay controls and behave as you would
expect; the four above are the ones with behavior worth knowing about.
