The Sufami Turbo is a 1996 Bandai adapter that goes into a Super Famicom's cartridge slot
and takes **two small cartridges of its own**. It is the only unit in RetroXR with more
than one bay, and the reason a bay is addressed by number at all.

## Two slots, and why they matter

The second slot is not a spare. **Nine of the thirteen Sufami Turbo games link the
cartridge in slot B into the game in slot A**: the six SD Gundam Generation titles lend
each other their units, and SD Ultra Battle lends its characters across the pair. A
one-slot Sufami Turbo would be the wrong machine rather than a simplified one.

Put the adapter into the Super Famicom's slot, then drop a cartridge into each well. One
cartridge on its own is a **first-class configuration**, not half a pair — the core sniffs
the first cartridge and maps slot B empty. Poi Poi Ninja World runs that way.

See [expansion hardware](/guide/playing/expansion-hardware/) for how a stack is assembled.

## What you need

- **`STBIOS.bin`** in `libretro/system/snes9x/` — the adapter's own shell program. Until it
  is installed, the Sufami Turbo is not offered in the spawn menu at all.
- **snes9x**, which RetroXR selects for you when the adapter goes in.
- Your cartridge dumps in the `sufami_turbo` folder of your ROM library.

<Aside type="caution" title="Without the BIOS it does not quietly degrade — it refuses">
	A missing `STBIOS.bin` does not fall back to loading the cartridge as an ordinary Super
	Famicom game. The load is refused outright: zero frames, and a content-load failure. The
	spawn gate is there so you meet that as a missing file rather than as a black screen.
</Aside>

## Your dumps are named `.sfc`, not `.st`

Every Sufami Turbo dump in circulation carries the `.sfc` extension, the same as an
ordinary Super Famicom game. RetroXR therefore accepts **both `.st` and `.sfc`** under this
system — otherwise your cartridges would be filed under Super Nintendo and the adapter's
bays would refuse them.

<Aside type="tip" title="Keep them in their own folder">
	Because the extension does not distinguish them, what separates a Sufami Turbo cartridge
	from a Super Famicom game here is **which folder it is in**. Put the small cartridges in
	`roms/sufami_turbo/`.
</Aside>

## Both cartridges keep their saves

The adapter has no battery — the cartridges do — so each one saves independently, and
RetroXR gives slot B a save file of its own.

That took a fix. snes9x reports slot A's save RAM and the plain save RAM from the same
place, while slot B sits behind a **core-specific memory id** of its own. Reading only the
obvious one gave the same 16 KB whether one cartridge was in or two, so **a linked pair
kept only half its progress** — SD Ultra Battle said as much on every launch, reporting
that the B cassette's backup was not initialised.

A save is keyed to the **cartridge**, not to the slot it sits in, so a game carries its
save between the two wells, and lending it to a different pairing does not overwrite what
is on it.

## Verified with

Measured 2026-08-30 against the No-Intro set: **SD Ultra Battle: Ultraman Densetsu** in
slot A with **Seven Densetsu** in slot B, running 767 frames at 256×224 — with the game
itself reporting the B cassette's backup state, which is the proof the link is live. A game
that could not see slot B would have nothing to say about it.
