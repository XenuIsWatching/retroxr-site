Holding an imaginary controller is charming for about ten minutes. If you have a real
gamepad, keyboard or mouse — Bluetooth or USB — RetroXR can forward it into a machine, and
your hands stay free.

The way it does that is a **receiver**: a small box on a one-meter lead that seats in a
controller port and forwards exactly one real device to it. Put the Quest controllers down
and the room still works while the game runs on the hardware in front of you.

<Aside type="tip" title="The object is the binding">
	There is no menu of devices, no capture mode, and nothing to arbitrate. **Seat a
	receiver to start, unplug it to stop.** What is plugged in is the whole configuration,
	and you can see it from across the room.
</Aside>

## The three receivers

Spawn them from `SPAWN` → **Controllers**. They are color-coded, and each carries a glyph
for what it forwards.

<Image src={controllersTab} alt="The Controllers tab of the spawn menu, listing a primitive controller, ray gun, mouse and keyboard — with no receivers, because no device was connected." />

<Aside type="caution" title="A receiver only appears once its device is connected">
	The list above has **no receivers in it** — that screenshot was taken with nothing
	plugged in. The Controllers tab is rebuilt from what is actually connected right now,
	so a gamepad receiver only appears once RetroXR can see a gamepad.

	If you cannot find the receiver you want, connect the device first, then reopen the
	tab.
</Aside>

### The LED

Every receiver has a small **lit LED** on its body. That is the whole status display:
seated and live, or not. It is deliberately a light on an object rather than a mode
indicator in a menu, because an object you can see across the room is easier to find again
than a setting you toggled.

### Gamepad

<Turntable
	src="/video/receivers/pad_receiver.mp4"
	poster={padPoster}
	alt="The pad receiver rotating: a gray box with a gamepad glyph and a name label on its top face."
	caption="The pad receiver. Its top face names the gamepad it forwards — and reads NO PAD when nothing is paired."
/>

A gamepad receiver forwards **one named gamepad**, chosen when it was spawned. That is why
the label matters: with two pads connected you spawn two receivers, seat them in ports 1
and 2, and get genuine two-player with nobody holding a virtual controller.

Connect the pad to your headset or PC first — the Controllers tab detects what is
connected and offers a receiver for it. The one in the picture reads **NO PAD** because
nothing was paired when it was rendered; normally it carries the name of the gamepad it
forwards.

### Keyboard

<Turntable
	src="/video/receivers/keyboard_receiver.mp4"
	poster={kbPoster}
	alt="The keyboard receiver rotating: a tan box with keyboard and Bluetooth glyphs on its top face."
	caption="The keyboard receiver. Seat it in a computer's port and type — no capture key, no mode."
/>

There is only one keyboard, so there is nothing to choose. Seat the receiver in a
computer's port and type.

This is deliberately simpler than the virtual keyboard you can pick up and hold, which
needs **Scroll Lock** pressed before your typing reaches the core. Holding a keyboard is
ambiguous — you might merely be carrying it — but plugging a receiver in is not. **Seated
is live**, and the box has a lit LED on it, which is easier to find again than a mode you
toggled with a key.

### Mouse

<Turntable
	src="/video/receivers/mouse_receiver.mp4"
	poster={mousePoster}

	caption="The mouse receiver. The mouse already on your desk, pointing at the machine."

The virtual mouse is a shell you slide across a surface in VR. It is a lovely object and a
poor pointing device — it needs a hand, a flat surface and your attention. The receiver
feeds the same port from the mouse already on your desk.

## Plugging one in

<Steps>

1. Connect the device to your headset or PC as you normally would.

2. Open `SPAWN` → **Controllers** and spawn the matching receiver.

3. Push its plug into one of the machine's **controller ports**, the same way you would
   seat a controller lead. See [plugging things in](/guide/playing/plugging-in/).

4. That is it. Nothing is held, and the LED shows it is live.

</Steps>

To stop, pull the receiver out of the port.

## Two of the same thing is a fan-out, not a conflict

Seat two keyboard receivers in two different machines and **both** get your keystrokes.
That is not a bug or an ambiguity — there is one real keyboard, and seating two receivers
is exactly how you asked for both. It is what a splitter does.

You cannot type at two machines at once in any meaningful sense, so nothing has to be
arbitrated.

## Handhelds have no ports

A handheld has nowhere to plug a receiver in. Choose the pad from **that machine's own
Controllers panel** instead — point at the handheld and click the left thumbstick.

## Desktop mode does not use receivers

<Aside type="caution" title="VR only">
	Receivers exist for VR, and the keyboard and mouse ones are offered in VR only.

	On desktop, **your keyboard and mouse are the player** — `WASD` walks and the mouse
	looks around. A dongle silently taking the keyboard would fight the controls you are
	standing on, and gating mouse-look would stop you turning. Desktop keeps the held
	keyboard with its Scroll Lock capture, and the held mouse shell, untouched.
</Aside>

A real gamepad plugged into the PC still works in desktop mode — see
[desktop controls](/guide/reference/desktop-controls/).
