Nothing in RetroXR is wired for you. A console reaches a television because you ran a
cable between them, and if you run it to the wrong socket you get the wrong result — not
an error message.

## Seating a plug

Grab the plug — not the cable — with **grip**, bring it to a socket, and it snaps in when
it is close enough and roughly lined up.

<Turntable
	src="/video/av/plug_seat.mp4"
	poster={seatPoster}
	alt="A yellow composite plug travels along the socket axis into the Composite 1 VIDEO input on a television's AV IN panel, then withdraws."
	caption="A composite video plug going into Composite 1. The plug snaps the last few millimeters once it is close and lined up."
/>

A few things that trip people up:

- **Plugs fall.** Let go of one in mid-air and it drops, swinging on its cable. If it
  lands near a panel of sockets it can seat itself in one of them — which is how a picture
  lead ends up in the wrong input without you touching it.
- **Sockets are close together.** A TV's input panel has several video sockets within a
  couple of centimeters of each other. Aim deliberately.
- **Pull a plug out** by grabbing it and moving it away from the socket.

The cables are simulated, not decorative — they hang, swing, and take up slack. Give
yourself room.

Each kind of lead ends in a connector that only fits its own socket. Plug on the left,
the socket it goes into on the right.

## AV IN: the television's panel

The sockets live on the back of the set, each block labeled with what it is.

<Image src={avPanel} alt="The back panel of a television: four blocks labeled Composite 1 to Composite 4, each with yellow video and white and red audio sockets marked AV IN, and a separate Antenna block with a single RF IN socket." />

**Four composite inputs**, so you can leave several machines wired up at once instead of
swapping cables. Cycle between them with the **source** button on the set, or on the
[TV remote](/guide/room/tv-remote/). The set shows the input it is switched to; the others
keep running.

There is also an **aspect ratio** button, switching the set between 16:9 and 4:3.

## AV OUT: the machine's panel

The console end is the mirror image — a row of phono sockets on the back, sometimes
alongside an RF output.

<Image src={consoleCart} alt="A console stand-in: a gray box with a RESET button, a green START button, four numbered controller ports, and a cartridge seated in the top slot." />

<Aside type="note" title="Not every machine has phono out">
	Older consoles predate composite sockets entirely and reach a television through an RF
	switch instead. The Wii goes the other way and uses a single **AV Multi Out** connector
	that fans out to phono — see [the Wii page](/guide/platforms/wii/).
</Aside>

## Composite: the normal way

Most consoles come with a **composite** lead: the familiar yellow-white-red trio, video
plus stereo audio.

1. Grab the plug at the console end of the lead if it is not already attached.
2. Push it into the matching row of phono sockets on the back of the TV.
3. Set the TV to that input.

Some machines only put out **mono composite** — video plus a single audio channel. It
seats in the same row; there is simply one fewer plug.

## RF: the antenna way

Older machines feed the television through an **RF switch** rather than a phono row.

<Turntable
	src="/video/av/rf_switch.mp4"
	poster={rfPoster}
	alt="The RXR-003 RF switch rotating: a small white box with a coaxial lead and a phono lead coming out of it."
	caption="The RXR-003 RF switch. Phono in from the console, coax out to the television's antenna socket."
/>

The chain is:

```
console → RF switch → TV antenna socket
```

Then tune the television to the channel the switch outputs on. On a channel with nothing
on it you get **snow**, exactly as you would have in 1988 — that is not a bug, it means
the set is working and the machine is not reaching it.

A console can feed composite and RF at the same time. Both outputs are live; which one you
see depends on the input the TV is showing.

## VGA and monitors

A **CRT computer monitor** takes a DE-15 (VGA) lead and has no phono row at all. If you
are staring at a monitor wondering where to put a composite plug, that is why — it takes a
different cable, and machines that can drive it have a VGA output of their own.

## Audio

Composite carries its own audio. Where a machine has a separate **3.5 mm** output, run
that to a speaker pair or to the set, or you will have a picture and silence.

The television's own volume and mute controls act on whatever input it is currently
showing — so a source you cannot hear may simply not be the selected input.

## Live television

If you have an **HDHomeRun** tuner on your network with an antenna attached, RetroXR finds
it automatically. Set the TV's source to the tuner and the channel up and down buttons
cycle your actual local channels.

## When there is no picture

Work down this list — it is ordered by how often each one is the answer.

1. **The plug is not seated**, or is seated in the neighboring socket.
2. **The TV is showing a different input.**
3. **The TV is switched off.** Its own power button, not the console's.
4. **The machine is off**, or has no cartridge or disc in it.
5. **The channel is untuned**, if you went through an RF switch — snow means exactly this.
6. **No core is installed** for that system, so there is nothing to produce a picture.

A blank input paints blank. That is deliberate: the television behaves like a television,
so the state of your wiring is always readable from the screen.
