<div class="rxr-lede">

RetroXR turns emulation into something you handle. Instead of a menu, you get a room: a
console on the floor, a CRT on the stand, cartridges on the shelf.

Spawn a system and a TV, run the A/V cable between them, push a cartridge into the slot
and hit power. The picture comes up on the TV. Everything is physical — cables hang and
swing, lids hinge open, discs sit in trays, and a cartridge has to go in the right way up.

</div>

## What's in the room

<FeatureGrid>
	<Feature title="Consoles you plug in" img={objConsoleCart} alt="A console stand-in: a gray box with a RESET button, a green START button, four numbered controller ports, and a cartridge seated in the top slot.">
		Spawn a system, seat a cartridge, run the A/V lead to a TV and hit power. Wire it wrong and you get a blank input — because that is what would happen.
	</Feature>
	<Feature title="Cores, downloaded in-headset" img={coresScrolled} alt="The core downloader: a scrollable grid of systems, each showing how many cores are available.">
		Powered by libretro, so it plays what libretro plays — NES, SNES, Mega Drive, PlayStation, Saturn, Dreamcast, GameCube, Game Boy, DS, 3DS, PSP and plenty more. No setting anything up on a PC first.
	</Feature>
	<Feature title="A default core per system" img={coresManager} alt="The core manager: a grid of systems, each labeled with its currently selected core.">
		Some systems have several cores with different strengths. Pick the one each platform launches with, and set its options, without leaving the headset.
	</Feature>
	<Feature title="BIOS files, tracked" img={coresBiosPsx} alt="The BIOS view for PlayStation, listing the PS1 BIOS files with their required or optional status and per-file download buttons.">
		The systems that need a BIOS say so, file by file, and mark which are required and which are optional — instead of failing to boot with no explanation.
	</Feature>
	<Feature title="A DVD player that plays discs" img={objDvd} alt="The DVD player stand-in: a black deck with a disc slot, transport buttons, and a menu navigation cluster.">
		Real disc menus and chapter navigation, driven by libVLC. Drop an ISO or a VIDEO_TS folder in, spawn a player and a TV, and wire them together.
	</Feature>
	<Feature title="A VCR for your own tapes" img={objVcr} alt="The VCR stand-in: a black deck with a tape slot, a glowing counter display, and transport buttons.">
		Your video files become VHS tapes you push into a deck. Play, pause, rewind, fast-forward — and it renders onto whichever TV it is plugged into.
	</Feature>
	<Feature title="Books you actually turn" video="/video/book_flip.mp4" poster={bookPoster} alt="A hand grabs the outer edge of a page and drags it across, folding it over to reveal the next spread." wide>
		PDFs and CBZs are physical books. Grab a page by its outer edge and drag it across; release past halfway and the turn completes, short of it and it springs back.
	</Feature>
	<Feature title="Link cables that carry real data" img={gcGbaLinkPoster} alt="A GameCube game on the television with two Game Boy Advance handhelds cabled to it on the desk, each showing its own screen." wide>
		Run a lead between two machines and they talk — Game Boy to Game Boy, PlayStation to PlayStation, and a GameCube to up to four Game Boy Advances, cartridge or single-pak. Two consoles, two cores, one wire.
	</Feature>
	<Feature title="Add-ons you stack by hand">
		A Sega CD under a Mega Drive, a 32X in its slot, a 64DD, a Jaguar CD, a Super Game Boy, a Sufami Turbo taking two cartridges at once. Build the stack and the right core is chosen for you.
	</Feature>
	<Feature title="One remote for all of it" img={objRemote} alt="The TV remote, held in a hand, with a floating command menu above it: eject, a D-pad with a center OK, transport buttons, and audio and subtitle cells.">
		Point it at a TV, VCR, DVD player or tape deck and the right controls appear above it — including the disc's own menus.
	</Feature>
</FeatureGrid>

<Aside type="note" title="The two stereoscopic machines look flat in a recording">
	A headset capture is one flat image, so the Virtual Boy and the 3DS lose the very thing
	that makes them worth playing here. Both are **genuinely stereoscopic in VR**: the core
	renders a separate image per eye, and RetroXR feeds the left half to your left eye and the
	right half to your right. No parallax barrier, no sweet spot to hold your head in.

	See [Virtual Boy](/guide/platforms/virtual-boy/) and
	[DS and 3DS](/guide/platforms/ds-and-3ds/).
</Aside>

## Bring your own everything

Copy ROMs across over a web interface on your local network and they show up in the menu,
ready to spawn into your room, with box art and scanned manuals if you have them.

Already running [RomM](https://romm.app)? RetroXR connects straight to your server. Browse
your whole collection from inside the headset, pull down just the games you want to play,
and let your saves sync back — no cable, no shuffling files by hand.

The room does more than games, too. A working VCR and DVD player take your own video
files, a CD player and tape deck handle music, and PDF manuals are books you can hold and
page through.

<div class="rxr-caveats">

## This is early days

RetroXR is **in active development**, and it is honest to say so up front:

- Most consoles and controllers are clean **procedural stand-ins** rather than detailed
  replicas — fully playable, deliberately minimalist. A growing handful — the NES, the
  Atari 2600, the PlayStation and its two pads, the Wii Remote and Nunchuk — wear a real
  shell.
- **Every room is a placeholder** — somewhere to put your hardware, not a finished space.
- Tested on **Quest 3**. There is also a Windows desktop build with mouse and keyboard.
- **Netplay is in, but it is very early.** Treat it as a prototype rather than a feature.
- RetroXR ships no games and no BIOS files. You bring your own.

</div>

## Help wanted — especially modelers

The biggest gap in RetroXR is **art**, not code. Every grey box is a console someone could
model properly, and the rooms are barely started.

If you model, this is where a contribution goes furthest — a single good room or console
shell changes the whole feel of the app. Two rules, both firm:

- Models must be **free of trademarked text and images**.
- They must be licensed so the project can ship them.

Not a modeler? Bug reports with logs, documentation fixes, and testing on hardware other
than a Quest 3 are all genuinely useful.

Come and say hello on **[Discord](https://discord.gg/mdjdBDTdW)**, or open an issue on
**[GitHub](https://github.com/XenuIsWatching/RetroXR/issues)**.
